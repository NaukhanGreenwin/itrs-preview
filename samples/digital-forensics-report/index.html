2Background and instructions

2.1 The client is an Ontario professional services firm. The Custodian held a senior advisory role with access to the firm’s client file share and a firm-managed Microsoft 365 account. The Custodian gave notice on Friday 28 August 2026 and confirmed an intention to join a competing firm.

2.2 On 8 September 2026 counsel instructed ITRS to preserve and examine the Custodian’s firm-issued devices and accounts. ITRS acted on counsel’s instructions throughout; this report is prepared for the purpose of anticipated litigation and is subject to legal privilege.

2.3 ITRS has no prior relationship with the Custodian and no interest in the outcome of the matter. The examiners’ duty in preparing this report is set out in section 9.

3Scope and questions examined

3.1 The examination covered the period 19 August 2026 00:00 to 4 September 2026 23:59 (Eastern Time), being the fourteen days before notice and the notice period. Activity outside that window was reviewed only where it explained an in-scope event.

3.2 Counsel asked ITRS to address the following questions:

  1. Q1Was firm data copied from firm systems to external storage media during the review period?
  2. Q2Was firm data transferred to personal cloud storage or personal email accounts?
  3. Q3Were steps taken to delete firm data or to conceal user activity?
  4. Q4Does the firm-issued mobile device hold firm documents or evidence of their transfer?
  5. Q5Can the files involved be identified individually for counsel’s review?

3.3 Out of scope: personal devices and accounts of the Custodian, which were not made available; review of document content for privilege or relevance, which remains with counsel; and any opinion on the Custodian’s intent, which is a matter for the trier of fact.

5Methodology and tools

5.1 The examination followed ITRS’s documented procedures, which are aligned with ISO/IEC 27037 (identification, collection, acquisition and preservation of digital evidence) and NIST SP 800-86 (integrating forensic techniques into incident response). Examiners kept contemporaneous notes for every step (Exhibit G).

01

Preserve

Write-blocked physical imaging of storage; full-file-system mobile extraction; read-only cloud collection. No examination was performed on original media.

02

Verify

SHA-256 computed at acquisition and recomputed on every working copy before analysis. All values matched (section 4).

03

Analyze

Artefacts parsed and correlated across sources: USB device history, file-system and link-file records, browser history, cloud audit events and mobile application data.

04

Validate

Each finding was confirmed by at least two independent artefacts or tools, and reviewed by a second examiner before inclusion.

7Key findings

7.1 Each finding is stated with the evidence on which it rests and a confidence level defined in Appendix B.

F1

Client files were copied to a USB storage device on 27 August 2026.

High

A USB device not issued by the firm was connected for 52 minutes. Link files and ShellBags record folders under E:\Clients being opened during that session, and the file server logged reads of the same 1,284 files by the Custodian’s account at matching times. 212 of the files belong to client folders not assigned to the Custodian.

Exhibit C, D
F2

Firm files were probably uploaded to a personal cloud storage service on 28 August 2026.

Moderate

Cached pages show the upload screen of a consumer cloud storage service signed in to a personal account, and a Jump List records a file-picker opened on E:\Clients. Browser history for the period was later cleared (F3), so the number of files uploaded cannot be determined.

Exhibit D
F3

Browser history was deliberately cleared on 3 September 2026.

High

Chrome preference records show a manual “clear browsing data” action with an “all time” range at 17:20. Firm policy does not clear browser data automatically. ITRS expresses no view on the reason for the action.

Exhibit D
F4

412 OneDrive files were deleted on the last working day; all were recovered.

High

The Unified Audit Log records 412 FileDeleted events in six minutes, followed by the first-stage recycle bin being emptied. All files were restored from the second-stage recycle bin and hash-matched to earlier versions.

Exhibit E
F5

No firm email was forwarded to personal accounts.

High

No inbox or transport forwarding rules existed in the review period, and no messages were sent from the mailbox to personal domains.

Exhibit E
F6

No firm documents were found on the mobile device.

Moderate

No firm documents, file-share paths or cloud-storage transfers were identified. One third-party messaging app stores content in an encrypted container that the extraction could not fully parse (section 8).

Exhibit F

8Limitations and assumptions

  1. 8.1The USB device used on 27 August has not been produced. Its current contents, and whether files were later copied elsewhere, cannot be determined from the evidence examined.
  2. 8.2Browser data cleared on 3 September could be recovered only in part. Finding F2 relies on cached pages and Jump List records, not on a complete browsing history.
  3. 8.3One third-party messaging application on E02 stores content in an encrypted container. Message metadata was available; message content and attachments were not.
  4. 8.4File server logs were produced by firm IT. ITRS observed the export and hashed it on receipt, but did not have direct access to the server.
  5. 8.5Attribution of activity to the Custodian rests on the Custodian’s credentials, device possession records and the absence of other interactive logons in the review period. ITRS cannot identify who was physically at the keyboard.
  6. 8.6This report reflects the evidence available on 25 September 2026. ITRS reserves the right to revise its conclusions if further evidence, including the USB device, becomes available.
