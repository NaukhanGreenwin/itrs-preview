1Executive summary

On 23 September 2026 a customer of the client telephoned to confirm a request, received by email from the client’s accounts payable lead (“User A”), to send payments to a new bank account. The client had sent no such request. External counsel retained ITRS the same morning to contain the incident and to establish how the mailbox was compromised, what was accessed and who was affected.

ITRS collected and hash-verified fourteen evidence items and examined the 72-hour window from 21 September 12:00 UTC to 24 September 12:00 UTC. Our answers to counsel’s questions are summarized below and detailed in section 7.

Q1
How did the attacker gain access?
An adversary-in-the-middle phishing page captured User A’s password and session token on 22 Sep at 09:21; the token was replayed from attacker infrastructure one minute later (F1).
High
Q2
When did unauthorized access begin and end?
Began 22 Sep 09:22 ET. Ended 23 Sep 10:52 ET when sessions were revoked; later attempts failed (F1, F2).
High
Q3
What information was accessed?
1,146 mail items were read or synced. 38 contain personal information of 27 individuals; no file-share or OneDrive access (F4).
Moderate
Q4
Who outside the client was affected?
Three customers received payment-redirection emails. One payment of $48,600 was made and recalled in full (F5).
High
Q5
Were other accounts compromised, and is access removed?
No other account shows attacker sign-ins, rules or consent grants. All persistence was removed by 23 Sep 11:30 ET (F6).
High

Containment was complete within 2 hours of ITRS’s instruction. The root cause was a phishing technique that defeats push-based multi-factor authentication; the remediation in section 8 addresses it directly.
